# Interview Script — read aloud, almost verbatim

**How to use this:** print it or keep it on screen. Read the sentences as written — they're written for the ear, not the eye. The bracketed stage directions are for you, not to be read out. Questions marked **★** are essential: if the call is being cut short, skip everything unmarked and get the ★ ones.

**The one skill that matters:** after you ask a question, **stop talking**. Count three seconds in your head. People fill silence with the truth. The biggest interviewing mistake is rescuing them from a pause by suggesting an answer — never do it.

**What never to do:** don't pitch, don't describe the product idea until the very end (and only if asked), don't say "so it must be frustrating that…" — leading the witness produces polite agreement, not data.

---

## Opening (about 1 minute)

> "Thanks so much for making time. Just to say up front again: I'm not selling anything today — there's genuinely nothing to sell. I'm doing research to work out whether a problem is real before anyone builds software for it, and you're the expert here, not me.
>
> Everything you tell me stays anonymous — if I write anything up it'll be 'a provider in [state]' at most, never your name or business name.
>
> Is it alright if I take notes as we talk?"

[If you want to record instead of typing:] 

> "Would you be comfortable if I recorded this, just so I don't have to scribble while you talk? I'll delete it once I've written up my notes, and no one else hears it."

[If they hesitate at all: "No problem at all, I'll just take notes." Never push. Recording a call without consent is illegal in several Australian states — consent on tape or no recording, ever.]

> "First, so I understand who I'm talking to —"

## Section 1 — Context (about 3 minutes)

**★ Q1.** "Can you tell me a bit about the organisation — what supports you deliver, and roughly how many staff?"

**★ Q2.** "And what's your role in it — in particular, who actually does the NDIS claiming? Is that you?"

[If claiming is someone else's job, ask:] "Would it be worth me talking to them too at some point?" [Log the referral.]

**Q3.** "Roughly how many participants do you support at the moment?" [For plan managers: "Roughly how many invoices a month do you process?"]

## Section 2 — The claiming pain (about 7 minutes — the heart of the call)

**★ Q4.** "Walk me through what happens between a support being delivered and the money arriving in your account. Just the actual steps, as they really happen."

[Shut up and let them talk. This one question often answers three others. Follow-ups if needed: "Who does that step?" "How long does that take?" "What software is that in?"]

**★ Q5.** "In the last twelve months, have you had claims rejected, cancelled, or paid and then clawed back?"

[If yes — go slow here, this is the gold:]
- "Tell me about the most recent one. What happened?"
- "How did you find out?"
- "What was the reason, as far as you could tell?"
- "How long did it take to sort out, and did you get the money in the end?"
- "Roughly how often does that happen — weekly, monthly, a few times a year?"

[If no:] "What do you put that down to — is someone checking things before they go in?" 

**★ Q6.** "Before a claim goes in, does anything or anyone check it against the participant's plan and the price limits — or does it go in and you find out?"

[Vague answer? Follow up:] "So if a support worker delivered something outside the plan, when would you find out about it?"

**Q7.** "When the NDIS price limits change — the price guide updates every July — what do you have to do on your end?"

## Section 3 — Audit and evidence (about 4 minutes)

**★ Q8.** "Have you been through an audit — certification or mid-term — in the last couple of years? What was getting ready for it actually like?"

[Follow-ups: "How long did preparation take?" "Who did it?" "Did you pay anyone external to help?" "What was the worst moment?"]

**Q9.** "If an auditor asked you tomorrow to show the evidence behind last month's claims — service records, agreements, worker screening — how long would that take you to pull together?"

## Section 4 — Money and tools (about 3 minutes)

**★ Q10.** "What software does the business currently pay for — rostering, care management, accounting, anything — and roughly what does it cost a month, all in?"

[People often don't total it up. Help them list: "So there's [X]… anything else? Xero? Anything for rostering?" Then: "So roughly what, per month, altogether?" Get a number, even a rough one.]

**Q11.** "Of the things we've talked about — rejected claims, checking before submission, audit prep — which one, if it disappeared tomorrow, would matter most to the business?"

**Q12.** "Is there anything about claiming or audits I haven't asked about that I should have?"

## Closing (about 1 minute)

> "This has been genuinely useful — thank you. Two last things.
>
> If, down the track, something gets built that checks claims before they're submitted and keeps audit evidence in order — would you want to hear about it? No commitment, just a yes or no.
>
> And is there anyone else you know — another provider, a plan manager — who'd have opinions on this? I'm trying to talk to about ten people."

[Log both answers. An unprompted "can I try it?" anywhere in the call is the strongest signal there is — mark it prominently in the notes.]

> "Thanks again — I'll let you get on with your day."

[If at any point they ask "so what are you building?" — answer honestly and briefly, *after* their answers are already given:] 

> "The idea being tested is a tool that checks every claim against the participant's plan, budget and price limits before it's submitted — so rejections get caught before they cost you money — and that keeps the audit evidence organised as you go. But whether it gets built depends on conversations like this one."

---

## If the call gets cut to 10 minutes

Do, in order: opening consent line → Q1, Q2 → Q4, Q5, Q6 → Q8 → Q10. That's every ★. Skip everything else, close warmly, and ask if you can follow up by email for the rest.
